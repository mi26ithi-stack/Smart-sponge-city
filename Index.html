🔬 Our NCSC Research Journey

The digital project follows the same scientific logic used in our research.

01 Problem

Urban rainwater can become surface runoff and create waterlogging.

02 Hypothesis

If water is captured, infiltrated, stored and monitored intelligently, runoff pressure can be reduced.

03 Methodology

Compare conventional and Sponge City pathways using measured parameters and a model.

04 Observation

Record rainfall, runoff, infiltration, storage, pond depth and rate of rise.

05 Analysis

Organise the observations into tables, formulas and graphs to identify patterns.

06 Solution

Use recharge, storage, detention and controlled overflow with monitoring and decision support.

Water management

Reduce uncontrolled runoff by combining infiltration, storage and detention.

Risk awareness

Changing water conditions can be treated as measurable signals rather than only visual observations.

Decision support

A simple rule-based model can convert measurements into NORMAL, CAUTION, WARNING and CRITICAL states.

Human safety

Warnings and safer routing can be included as part of urban water management.

Research value

Field observations can replace the illustrative values in this prototype when genuine readings are collected.

Future scope

Real sensors, GIS, weather data and a secure AI service could make the system more advanced.

Project Framework

Problem

Impervious surfaces can increase runoff and drainage pressure while reducing natural infiltration.

Solution

Permeable surfaces, rain gardens, recharge areas, storage and detention manage water closer to where rain falls.

Smart Layer

IoT measurements can monitor rainfall, soil moisture, water level, pond depth and rate of rise.

Future

Weather forecasting, GIS mapping, groundwater and water-quality monitoring, automation and networked stations.

Core flow

Rainfall → Runoff/Infiltration → Storage/Recharge → Monitoring → Risk Assessment → Intelligent Response

Slide 01 • Why this matters

When water becomes a safety problem

Urban waterlogging is not only a drainage issue. When depth, flow and hazards are difficult to judge, ordinary movement around roads and ponds can become risky.

Risk → Detection → Response
🌧️

Heavy rainfall

Rainfall can generate rapid surface runoff, especially where surfaces are less permeable.

💧

Hidden conditions

Water may hide changes in depth, open drains, slippery surfaces or unsafe edges.

⚠️

Delayed response

If people do not receive an early warning, they may unknowingly enter an unsafe area.

Slide 02 • Safety scenarios

Three situations. One smart idea.

These are conceptual safety demonstrations for the exhibition. They show why detection and early warnings matter; they do not reproduce real accidents.

🏞️

Unknown pond depth

A person approaches a pond without knowing the depth or whether the edge is safe.

Smart response: level/depth monitoring + visible warning + restricted unsafe zone.

🛣️

Waterlogged road

Standing water can hide road conditions and make a route difficult to assess.

Smart response: water-level and rate-of-rise monitoring + alternate-route alert.

🚧

Hidden drainage hazard

Poor visibility can make a drainage opening or damaged section difficult to notice.

Smart response: hazard mapping + detection + warning before the unsafe point.

Slide 03 • Smart decision system

From rainfall to a safer decision

Conventional pathway

🌧️ Rain→💧 Runoff→🚶 Person→⚠️ Risk

Limited information can mean that a person sees the hazard only after reaching it.

Smart Sponge pathway

🌧️ Rain→📡 Sense→🧠 Analyse→🚨 Warn

Measurements support earlier decisions: recharge, store, detain, warn or redirect.

Decision rule

Normal: recharge and normal storage → Caution: recharge + storage → Warning: storage + detention → Critical: controlled overflow and safety warning.

Slide 04 • Research methodology

Experimental Testing Methodology

The proposed system can be evaluated through structured observation, measurement, comparison and analysis of the identified water-management components.

01 • SurveyIdentify conventional surfaces, pond, garden, recharge and storage locations.
02 • RecordNote rainfall, water depth, runoff behaviour and time changes.
03 • CompareCompare conventional and Sponge City pathways.
04 • AnalyseUse formulas such as rate of pond rise = depth change ÷ time change.
05 • ConcludeIdentify where capture, infiltration, storage or warning can improve the system.
